City Council Approves Riverfront Park Expansion After Months of Debate

By Jane Reporter · Published June 6, 2026

The city council voted 6 to 1 on Tuesday night to approve a long-debated expansion of Riverfront Park, clearing the way for twelve additional acres of green space along the eastern bank of the Miller River. The decision capped nearly eighteen months of public hearings, revised budgets, and at times heated disagreement among residents.

Under the approved plan, the expansion will add a riverside walking trail, two playgrounds, and a small amphitheater intended to host summer concerts. Construction is expected to begin in the autumn and finish by late next year, according to the Parks and Recreation Department.

"This is a win for every family in our city," said council member Aisha Okoro, who sponsored the measure. "Green space is not a luxury. It is infrastructure, and it is health, and our residents have asked for it again and again."

The lone dissenting vote came from council member Tom Halvorsen, who argued that the projected eight million dollar cost should instead be directed toward repaving the city's aging road network. "I support parks," he said, "but I cannot support borrowing against next year's budget to build one while Fourth Street falls apart."

City planners said the project will be funded through a combination of a state recreation grant and municipal bonds, and that no increase to the local property tax rate is anticipated. A spokesperson for the mayor's office added that the new trail will connect to the existing downtown greenway, creating a continuous four mile path along the river.

Public reaction at the meeting was largely supportive. More than forty residents signed up to speak during the comment period, with most urging the council to move forward. A small number raised concerns about parking and nighttime noise from the amphitheater, which planners said would be addressed with a 9 p.m. event curfew.

With the vote complete, the city will now solicit bids from contractors. Officials expect to award the construction contract within ninety days and to hold a groundbreaking ceremony before the first frost.
